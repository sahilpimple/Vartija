## [nis2:art23-3] NIS2 Article 23(3): when an incident is "significant"
An incident counts as significant when it has caused, or could cause, severe disruption to the entity's services or financial loss for the entity. It is also significant when it has affected, or could affect, other people or organisations by causing considerable material or non-material damage. Implementing acts can set more precise thresholds for some digital-infrastructure sectors.

## [nis2:art23-4] NIS2 Article 23(4): the three-step reporting sequence
Without undue delay and within 24 hours of becoming aware of a significant incident, the entity sends an early warning. The early warning says whether the incident is suspected to be caused by unlawful or malicious acts and whether it could have a cross-border impact. Within 72 hours of becoming aware, the entity sends an incident notification that updates the early warning and gives an initial assessment of severity and impact, plus indicators of compromise where available. The authority can ask for intermediate status reports.

## [nis2:art23-4-final] NIS2 Article 23(4)(d): the final report
No later than one month after the incident notification, the entity submits a final report. It covers a detailed description of the incident including severity and impact, the type of threat or root cause that likely triggered it, the mitigation measures applied and ongoing, and any cross-border impact. If the incident is still ongoing at that point, a progress report is sent instead and the final report follows within a month of handling the incident.

## [nis2:art21] NIS2 Article 21: cybersecurity risk-management measures
Essential and important entities must take appropriate and proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures to manage cyber risk. The minimum list includes risk analysis and security policies, incident handling, business continuity and crisis management, supply-chain security, secure development and vulnerability handling, effectiveness assessment, cyber hygiene and training, cryptography, human-resources security and access control, and multi-factor authentication where appropriate.

## [nis2:art20] NIS2 Article 20: management accountability
The management bodies of essential and important entities must approve the cybersecurity risk-management measures, oversee their implementation, and can be held liable for infringements. Members of management bodies are required to follow cybersecurity training.
